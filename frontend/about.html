About LandGuard

Why we're teaching a model to read mountains.

Most landslides in North East India happen where no one is watching closely enough, fast enough. LandGuard exists to close that gap between when the ground starts moving and when someone finds out.

The problem

Steep terrain, heavy monsoon rainfall, and limited ground sensors make the region especially exposed to landslides — and especially hard to monitor by hand. By the time a slope failure is visible on the road, it's usually too late to warn anyone nearby.

Satellites, on the other hand, pass over the same ground every few days regardless of terrain or weather. LandGuard's job is to turn those repeat passes into an early warning, not just an after-the-fact record.

What the model is trained on

We combine optical and radar satellite imagery with elevation, slope, soil, and rainfall data, then train a machine learning model to recognise the conditions that precede a slide — not just the ones that follow one.

  • Optical imageryvegetation & surface change
  • SAR imageryground movement, all-weather
  • Elevation modelslope angle & aspect
  • Rainfall datagridded, historical & live

How risk is scored

Each monitored zone gets a probability of failure, refreshed as new imagery arrives, and grouped into three bands so field teams can triage at a glance.

Critical — act now
Moderate — watch closely
Stable — routine monitoring

LandGuard is a student-built project, and the model is still learning — every field report that confirms or corrects a prediction makes the next one sharper.
